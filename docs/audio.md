# Audio implementation contract

Sound and music remain planned in the [roadmap](roadmap.md). This document owns the implementation requirements when audio is introduced; it does not describe an existing playback system or request asset generation. [Assets](assets.md) owns source selection, preparation and publication. [Foundation](architecture.md#introduce-audio) owns integration steps.

Use one runtime playback owner with injectable media, clock and timer seams. Application boot/disposal owns its lifetime; session events supply resolved gameplay cues. Presentation audio never commits damage, progression, transitions or saves. Play success cues only for accepted outcomes, and keep rejected-action feedback distinct. Follow [player action feedback](game-design.md#player-action-feedback): essential meaning survives mute, missing media and failed playback.

One-shot cues have explicit pending, playing and finished lifetimes. Cancel pending timers on owner disposal or session replacement. Dispose media, handlers and timers through one idempotent path; late completion/failure callbacks cannot stop replacement sounds or clear a newer cooldown reservation. A delayed cue starts its cooldown when it actually starts. Cancelled or muted pending cues consume no cooldown; failed playback releases its own reservation. Decide pause behavior, voice limits and repeat suppression with the authored sound design rather than copying Alchemy's values.

Music and ambience have one requested destination and an explicit active owner. Superseded loads/fades cannot start late. Keep a usable outgoing track if a requested destination fails; unrelated invalidation must not stop playback. Choose crossfade, pause/resume and room-loop policies during implementation. Master/music/SFX settings update live playback consistently and stay separate from checkpoint writes. Muting must cancel inaudible pending work according to the selected policy and preserve a deliberate unmute path.

Playback failure is non-fatal: report bounded diagnostics while preserving controls and gameplay. Source/decode readiness cannot hold simulation progress hostage. Check representative stale-load, failed-play, mute/unmute, transition, pause and disposal cases at the cheapest meaningful layer. Listen in the actual Game and Dev Preview for overlaps, loop seams, levels, first use and cleanup. Passing lifetime tests cannot certify the mix. Record established behavior here when the system ships; keep exact tuning and registrations in their code/content owners.
